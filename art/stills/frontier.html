The large saving existed only in the charge model

Author · research · Rust control plane, Python verifier

Does deciding which attempt runs next get more independently verified results out of a fixed compute budget?

Stopping a verification at its first failing case cut its modelled charge by 92.9%, because the model billed per case. Then the verifications were timed. Almost all of the time was the call itself, which the model never billed.

1

The 199 cases, magnified

A verification costs about 120.5 ms before any case runs: process start-up, hidden-input generation, the harness. Each case adds about 0.05 ms.

Half of the failing candidates fail on their very first case. Even so, stopping there saves about 8 ms of 132.

What did not change: every verdict. All 1,257 candidates were judged the same both ways.
