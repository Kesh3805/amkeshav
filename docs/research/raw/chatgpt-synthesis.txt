Four deeper observations

The best interaction changes what the visitor knows. A camera zoom or hover effect changes pixels. A serious interaction changes the viewer's understanding, decision, or expectation. That's the difference between interaction as entertainment and interaction as explanation.

The most memorable sites compress their identity into a rule. Bruno Simon's vehicle, Ciechanowski's live mechanical models, and Nicky Case's playable systems work because the interaction mechanics are inseparable from the creator's point of view. Visual spectacle isn't the organizing principle.

The transition matters less than the state it reveals. A great transition moves the visitor from one understanding of a system to another. It should be possible to state the knowledge gained before and after each major scene.

Uncertainty is an underused design material. Most creative websites present immaculate finished outcomes. Research-oriented websites can make hypotheses, failed experiments, competing models, confidence ranges, and revised beliefs part of their experience. That's a particularly distinctive opportunity for a systems engineer.

The taxonomy I'd add to your library is therefore:

governing_mechanic, visitor_agency, state_transition, knowledge_gain, evidence_model, navigation_model, spatial_model, failure_visibility, live_vs_static, and fallback_mode.

That will make the corpus substantially more useful than categories such as 3D, minimal, cinematic, or WebGL alone.

4. One additional recommendation for the corpus

Your 547 cross-source sites are useful signals, but independent-source agreement measures discoverability, not originality.

A visually conventional agency website can appear in six galleries. A genuinely strange personal project may appear in only one obscure archive.

I'd keep two separate scores:

Source confidence

How well is the site documented, live-checked, attributed and independently discoverable?

Interaction originality

Does it introduce a genuinely distinctive governing idea, relationship between input and output, method of navigation, or way of understanding information?

And I'd add one mandatory research sentence for any site promoted into the top tier:

"The visitor does X, causing Y to change, which reveals Z."

For example, Red Blob Games: the visitor changes the pathfinding configuration, the algorithm's exploration changes, and its decision-making becomes visible.

If a researcher cannot complete that sentence with something meaningful, the site may still have extraordinary art direction, but it shouldn't automatically score highly for interaction originality.

That creates a distinction between beautiful, technically complex, and conceptually original—three qualities we've repeatedly had to separate in this project.

My final research priority: mine scientific instruments, investigative tools, Japanese/Korean domestic design archives, playable historical web projects, and independent browser games before adding more Awwwards or WebGL portfolio examples.

Those five areas are where I expect the largest number of ideas that aren't already represented by modern creative-development component libraries.
