## What it does

A deterministic Rust control plane runs research attempts under a fixed compute budget and accepts a result **only** through a verifier receipt. Allocation policies — round-robin, Thompson sampling with and without hints, greedy priors — compete for the same budget. Every campaign is logged to a hash-chained event log and can be replayed bit for bit.

## Why it exists

"Spend more compute, get more answers" hides two questions: *which* attempt deserves the next unit of budget, and how much of what you accept is actually right. FRONTIER exists to measure both at once, with a verifier in the loop rather than a model's opinion of itself.

## How it's built

- `frontier-core` — specs, attempt DAG, budget ledger, evidence store, hash-chained event log, a portable RNG and libm.
- `frontier-sched` — nine allocation policies.
- `frontier-sim` — environment, runner, audit; `frontier-cli` on top.
- A Python layer — property-test verifier, a deterministic task DSL worker, bootstrap analysis, a Docker sandbox.

## The most useful result so far

exp-008 showed that charging a failed verification only up to its first failing case recovered most of the yield a strong verifier cost — **in the charge model**. exp-009 timed it for real: **6.4% measured, against 92.9% modelled**. About 92% of a verification's cost is fixed interpreter overhead (≈120.5 ms + 0.05 ms per case). The verdicts were identical; the saving mostly wasn't there.

That one result invalidates the earlier modelled savings until they're re-run under measured costs. It's on the page because that's the point of running it.

## Tradeoffs

- Deterministic replay makes every claim checkable and every bug reproducible. It also meant writing a portable libm after the exp-000 headline values turned out to reproduce only on the Windows build.
- Pilots use a constructed task family, not a sealed benchmark.

## Where it stands

Simulation and deterministic-task pilots complete. No model-backed study has run: it needs a model, a provider and a spending cap. Next is re-running the allocation comparisons under the measured cost model.
